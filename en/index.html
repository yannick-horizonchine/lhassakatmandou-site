Lhasa to Kathmandu by Bike

18 September to 7 October 2027 — around twelve days in the saddle, around ten passes above 4,000m and about 9,000m of climbing, from the Potala Palace to Everest Base Camp, then across the Himalaya to Kathmandu.

Tibet → Nepal 18 Sep → 7 Oct 2027

This is not a simple crossing — it's a cycling journey across one of the highest, most spectacular plateaus on Earth. From Lhasa and the Potala Palace, around twelve days of riding along the Friendship Highway cross around ten passes above 4,000m — several of them above 5,000m, including the Gyagtso La at 5,220m — for about 9,000m of climbing. Along the way: Yamdrok Lake, the towns of Gyantse and Shigatse, Everest Base Camp at the foot of the north face, then the crossing of the Himalaya into Nepal and the temples of the Kathmandu valley. A demanding route, for trained cyclists who are at ease in the mountains.

This trip starts high and climbs fast: around twelve riding days between 3,400 and over 5,200m, with stages that can exceed 120km and 1,300m of ascent. Good acclimatisation is essential — the first days (Xining, then Lhasa) allow a gentle climb before the passes of the plateau, and Axel, your guide, is a medical correspondent of IFREMMONT: he can contact an altitude-medicine specialist at any time. Your Guide

Axel, Guide and Explorer

Your guide for the whole trip

Axel

Axel is a professional adventurer and outdoor leader. He has worked in demanding environments — the Alps, the Amazon, the Sahara — and has completed high-mountain expeditions, a dugout canoe crossing and desert expeditions. This experience has given him a deep, practical knowledge of life in the wild.

A former environmental and energy engineer, he worked at the Shanghai Astronomical Observatory. Drawn to Chinese history and culture, he studied Chinese and ancient Chinese history. Since 2024, he has been leading travellers on long-distance crossings in Europe, China and other countries. He has already guided in Tibet and has crossed China by bike.

He loves mountain adventure and wildlife watching, and enjoys sharing the cultural stories of the road with the group.

Languages: Chinese, English, French, Spanish
Health: medical correspondent of IFREMMONT (Institut de formation et de recherche en médecine de montagne)

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

The trip

What is the programme?

Lhasa to Kathmandu by Bike is a 20-day (19-night) trip, from 18 September to 7 October 2027. You meet in Xining (day 1) and the trip ends in Kathmandu (day 20). Around twelve days are spent in the saddle on the Friendship Highway, from the Potala Palace in Lhasa to Everest Base Camp, then across the Himalaya to Kathmandu.

Why start in Xining rather than Lhasa?

The trip starts in Xining (2,300m), in the north-east of the Tibetan plateau. From there, you reach Lhasa by overnight train (about 22 hours), the Tibet train, the highest in the world. This gentle arrival, full of scenery, also lets you gain altitude gradually.

What are the highlights?

Lhasa and the Potala Palace, the monasteries of Sera, Drepung and the Jokhang, Yamdrok Lake, the towns of Gyantse and Shigatse, around ten passes above 4,000m (several of them above 5,000m, including the Gyagtso La at 5,220m), Everest Base Camp with a night at Rongbuk Monastery, then Kathmandu and its valley (Durbar Square, Patan, Bhaktapur, Swayambhunath, Boudhanath).

What are the distance and the climbing?

Expect around twelve days of riding, about 9,000m of climbing in total, and stages that can exceed 120km and 1,300m of ascent. The day-by-day details (distance, ascent, night altitude) are given on this page, in the "Day by Day" section.

Why this time of year?

The 18 September departure comes after the monsoon and before the arrival of the big cold: it is the ideal season for this trip. The dates were also chosen so that we pass Everest Base Camp (Rongbuk, on 30 September) before the Chinese national holidays, from 1 to 7 October, and avoid the crowds.

Level and preparation

What level do I need?

The route is demanding: it is for trained cyclists who are comfortable in the mountains. The trip starts high and climbs fast, with around twelve riding days between 3,400 and over 5,200m.

Are all the passes hard?

No, the effort varies from day to day. Some stages have fairly easy passes (for example two small passes on day 10), others are longer and steeper (Karo La, Gyagtso La, the Nyalam Tongla climb). The last pass, the Yagru Xong La (5,125m), has an easy and spectacular descent.

Which bike is provided?

One mountain bike per person (Giant or equivalent), with spare parts, is included. Camping equipment is also provided, except your sleeping bag.

Can you help me prepare my acclimatisation?

Yes. As with our other very high-altitude routes, we can help you prepare your acclimatisation before departure. Please contact us to talk about it.

Altitude and safety

How is acclimatisation organised?

Good acclimatisation is essential. The first days of the trip (Xining at 2,300m, then Lhasa at 3,835m, with three nights there) allow a gentle climb before the passes of the plateau.

What happens if I have a health problem at altitude?

Your guide Axel is a medical correspondent of IFREMMONT (Institut de formation et de recherche en médecine de montagne) and can contact an altitude-medicine specialist at any time. In addition, oxygen, a portable hyperbaric chamber (PAC) and a full first-aid kit are available and travel with the team throughout the trip.

Is there a support vehicle?

Yes: a support minivan and a logistics vehicle accompany the group throughout the trip.

Who is Axel?

Axel is your guide for the whole trip. A professional adventurer and outdoor expedition leader, he has worked in the Alps, the Amazon and the Sahara. A former environmental and energy engineer, he studied Chinese and ancient Chinese history, has already guided in Tibet and has crossed China by bike. He speaks Chinese, English, French and Spanish.

Accommodation and meals

Where do we sleep?

In hotels in the cities, with 4 nights camping during the ride (near Quxu, near Nagarze, at Jingdingzhen and at Nyalam Tongla, at 4,800m). The night at Rongbuk, near Everest Base Camp, is in a guesthouse. The night of day 2 is spent on board the Tibet train.

How do the camp nights and meals work?

A cook and an assistant travel with you the whole way, with full camp kitchen equipment: nights in tents and hot meals, even at 4,800m. All camping equipment is provided, except your sleeping bag, which you bring.

Are all meals included?

All meals are included, except some listed as not included: lunches and dinners in Xining, Lhasa and Gyantse, and dinners in Kathmandu and Shigatse.
